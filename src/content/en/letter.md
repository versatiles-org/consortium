News sites, companies, public agencies, emergency alert services – countless services show maps to their users. Yet almost all of them rent those maps from a handful of large platforms. The result: high costs, dependency and lock-in. Once you are in, it is hard to get out.

> There is another way.

VersaTiles is an open map infrastructure – free, open-source and self-hostable. Broadcasters like **NDR and SWR** already host VersaTiles on their own infrastructure; more than [60 showcases across 17 countries](https://docs.versatiles.org/showcases/) show that it is in use worldwide. And the economics are striking: if you serve interactive maps to a large audience, VersaTiles is **a hundred to a thousand times cheaper** than commercial providers.

That makes VersaTiles something too rarely thought of as open: **digital public infrastructure**. Infrastructure like this should not sit in private hands, be exploited for profit, be sold off, or go bankrupt. It should be **broadly and publicly governed** – the way the **World Wide Web Consortium** keeps the web an open platform, or the way **DIN**, Germany's national standards body, uses standards to minimise lock-in across industry.

Infrastructure like VersaTiles needs broad support – from business, government and academia. That is exactly who we are looking for.

### The plan

- Together with **ten organisations, we found an association** (a German _Verein_) that stewards VersaTiles: it holds the rights, funds ongoing development, and decides on the direction together.
- **Each founding member contributes €700 net per month** (plus VAT). That secures development from day one. As the association grows, contributions can fall – the funding spreads across more shoulders.
- **Once the association has built reserves of more than €25,000**, it can found a hosting company. It offers map services to everyone, its profits flow back into the association – and it is protected by the association against hostile takeovers.

VersaTiles stays free for everyone to use; nobody has to become a member to run it. But building your maps on it without helping to carry it is a bet that somebody else will pay for its development. The consortium is the offer not to have to take that bet – and to help decide instead where the infrastructure you rely on is heading.

This is not a plea for a donation. It is an offer to invest in public digital infrastructure. That investment pays no dividend, but it does deliver lower costs, technical innovation and legal certainty for your own organisation. Together we can build a **serious alternative to the platform economy** – one that does not trap customers through lock-in or fleece them with excessive fees. Whoever co-founds it is not a customer, but a **co-owner and co-decision-maker** of infrastructure they already rely on.

I want to found the association **by 1 September**. Until then, I am looking for the first ten partners. If your organisation uses maps – or wants to, without becoming dependent – then let us talk.
